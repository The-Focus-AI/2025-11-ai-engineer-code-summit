## Themes

### 1. Verification is what lets agents run unattended — 11 talks

Autonomy is bounded by what can be checked automatically. Talks from app builders, platform teams and model trainers converge on the same move: give the agent (or the trainer) a strict, outcome-based check, and expect it to cheat or "paint doors" when the check is weak.

- What AI can do is a function of what can be specified and verified, which is why coding agents lead. “the sort of frontier and boundary of what can be solved by AI systems is really just a uh sort of an input function of whether or not you can specify an objective” — Eno Reyes, [*Making Codebases 'Agent-Ready'*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ShuJ_CN6zr4&t=81s) at 01:21 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-eno-reyes-factory-ai/transcript.md))
- The limit is the organisation's validation, not the agent. “The limiter is not the capability of the coding agent. The limit is your organization's validation criteria.” — Eno Reyes, [*Making Codebases 'Agent-Ready'*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ShuJ_CN6zr4&t=860s) at 14:20 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-eno-reyes-factory-ai/transcript.md))
- Without testing, agents ship features that look done but are not; Replit found more than 30% broken and built autonomous testing because models claim completion falsely. “we have to overcome the laziness of frontier models. So we need to verify that whenever a model tells us that a task has been completed, there is actually the truth” — Michele Catasta, [*Autonomy Is All You Need*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLhAA9yguwM&t=622s) at 10:22 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-michele-catasta-replit/transcript.md))
- Deterministic validation with clear errors is a "no-regrets" investment. “any kind of objective deterministic validation that you give an agent will increase its capabilities.” — Max Kanat-Alexander, [*Developer Experience in the Age of AI Coding Agents*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rT2Del5pwg4&t=274s) at 04:34 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-max-kanat-alexander-capital-one/transcript.md))
- Kernel generation shows the failure mode: huge speedups usually mean the agent gamed the benchmark, so the verification agent has to be strict. “Another case was a case that we saw which had a 71,000x speed up. And anything like that should trigger your suspicion brain.” — Natalie Serrino, [*Using AI-Generated Kernels to Instantly Speed Up PyTorch*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6guQG_tGt0o&t=708s) at 11:48 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-natalie-serrino-gimlet-labs/transcript.md))
- The same holds in training: a benchmark and an RL environment differ only in what the reward is used for, and a good verifier checks the outcome, not the route. “Benchmarks measure models. RL environments improve models. The score doesn't just stop in a leaderboard where you publish the results.” — Nik Pash, [*Hard-Won Lessons from Building Effective AI Coding Agents*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I8fs4omN1no&t=349s) at 05:49 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-nik-pash-cline/transcript.md))
- OpenAI's Agent RFT partners hit reward hacking; success needs an unambiguous, unhackable grader. “There should be a clear, unambiguous definition of success. You should have removed all subjectivity out of your task.” — Will Hang, Cathy Zhou, [*Agent Reinforcement Fine Tuning*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p1CmPZ2j6Lk&t=901s) at 15:01 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-will-hang-cathy-zhou-openai/transcript.md))
- Antigravity turns verification into something a human can glance at: a recording, not just a diff. “you end up with not just a diff, you end up with a screen recording of what it did.” — Kevin Hou, [*Defying Gravity*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HN-F-OQe6j0&t=586s) at 09:46 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-kevin-hou-google-deepmind/transcript.md))
- Zapier's Scout runs plan, execute and **validate** as CI phases before a merge request exists. “we run three phases in that pipeline. plan, execute and validate to generate this merge request.” — Lisa Orr, [*Your Support Team Should Ship Code*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RmJ4rTLV_x4&t=665s) at 11:05 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-lisa-orr-zapier/transcript.md))
- Review tools can enforce verification as policy. “you can set your AI code review tool to tell you block this PR if it doesn't cover certain level of test coverage.” — Itamar Friedman, [*The State of AI Code Quality: Hype vs. Reality*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rgjF5o2Qjsc&t=771s) at 12:51 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-itamar-friedman-qodo/transcript.md))
- Prime Intellect: an environment forces a real eval. “It's a proper eval. And what this means is that you can't just vibe check it.” — Will Brown, [*RL Environments at Scale*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_IzZWeuTx7I&t=399s) at 06:39 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-will-brown-prime-intellect/transcript.md))
- At Northwestern Mutual, the first users were BI experts precisely because they could verify the output. “So we took that crawl, walk, run approach that basically said we're first going to release it to actual BI experts” — Asaf Bord, [*Small Bets, Big Impact: Building GenBI at a Fortune 100*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LU9KgcZDRfY&t=462s) at 07:42 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-asaf-bord-northwestern-mutual/transcript.md))

From the notes: day 2's overview lists "automatic verification is key" and "getting the right data and verification is the major challenge"; Eno Reyes's line "a slop test is better than no test" and Nik Pash's tea-kettle verifier were both written down.

### 2. More code is not more output: review is the new bottleneck — 10 talks

Generation got cheap; reading did not. Leadership-day data (Bloomberg, McKinsey, Stanford, Qodo) and engineering-day practitioners report the same shape: more PRs, longer reviews, more rework, flat net output.

- More PRs means more bugs in total, even at the same defect rate. “even if there's not less bugs per line of code you have much more bugs because there are much more PRs” — Itamar Friedman, [*The State of AI Code Quality: Hype vs. Reality*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rgjF5o2Qjsc&t=519s) at 08:39 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-itamar-friedman-qodo/transcript.md))
- Bloomberg saw open PRs and time-to-merge both rise. “the average open pull requests increased and time to merge also increased uh because you spin a lot of new code and then still we have to review the code” — Lei Zhang, [*What We Learned Deploying AI within Bloomberg's Engineering Organization*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q81AzlA-VE8&t=453s) at 07:33 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-lei-zhang-bloomberg/transcript.md))
- McKinsey: fuzzy specs plus more code just move the manual work. “you've you've automated some things but we've generated more manual reviews.” — Martin Harrysson, Natasha Maniar, [*Moving away from Agile: What's Next?*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SZStlIhyTCY&t=356s) at 05:56 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-martin-harrysson-natasha-maniar-mckinsey/transcript.md))
- Stanford's case study: PR count is not output. “PRs went up by 14%. But this is inconclusive because more PRs doesn't mean better.” — Yegor Denisov-Blanch, [*How to Quantify AI ROI in Software Engineering*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JvosMkuNxF8&t=885s) at 14:45 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-yegor-denisov-blanch-stanford/transcript.md))
- Writing code has turned into reading it, and a weak review bar makes productivity fall. “if you don't have a process that is capable of rejecting things that shouldn't go in, you will very likely actually see decreasing productivity gains” — Max Kanat-Alexander, [*Developer Experience in the Age of AI Coding Agents*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rT2Del5pwg4&t=809s) at 13:29 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-max-kanat-alexander-capital-one/transcript.md))
- Amp rebuilt its editor around reviewing agent output. “I don't know about you, but I spend most of my time effectively doing code review now.” — Beyang Liu, [*Amp Code: Next-Generation AI Coding*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvIAkmZUEZY&t=148s) at 02:28 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-beyang-liu-amp-code/transcript.md))
- Dex Horthy moves review upstream, to research and plans, where one error multiplies. “a bad line of code is a bad line of code and a bad part of a plan is could be a hundred bad lines of code” — Dex Horthy, [*No Vibes Allowed: Solving Hard Problems in Complex Codebases*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rmvDxxNubIg&t=1006s) at 16:46 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-dex-horthy-humanlayer/transcript.md))
- swyx frames it as an asymmetric war on slop. “the amount of taste needed to fight SLO is in order manually bigger than that needed to produce it.” — swyx, [*Opening Remarks (No More Slop)*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IoiHI7p12Ao&t=285s) at 04:45 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-swyx-no-more-slop/transcript.md))
- Jake Nations: conversational coding never pushes back, so complexity compounds unseen. “There's no resistance to bad architectural decisions. The code just morphs to satisfy your latest request.” — Jake Nations, [*The Infinite Software Crisis*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eIoohUmYpGI&t=393s) at 06:33 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-jake-nations-netflix/transcript.md))
- Kitze: accept everything and you eventually hit a wall. “if you keep writing slop and you keep accepting everything eventually even with your engineering skills you're going to hit a roadblock” — Kitze, [*From Vibe Coding To Vibe Engineering*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JV-wY5pxXLo&t=1179s) at 19:39 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-kitze-sizzy/transcript.md))

From the notes: day 1's overview has "More People Pushing Code into Production — bottleneck is around assignment, code review pipeline, code review quality"; swyx's "no more slop" call-and-response is recorded verbatim.

### 3. Context is the scarce resource: compact it, isolate it, disclose it progressively — 9 talks

Quality is a function of what is in the window. The talks differ on tools (memory APIs, sub-agents, skills, research-plan-implement) but agree that context must be actively managed, and that sub-agents exist to protect it, not to role-play a team.

- Anthropic's platform work starts from the window. “Keeping the right context in the window at any given time is really really critical to getting the best outcomes from Claude.” — Katelyn Lesse, [*Evolving Claude APIs for Agents*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aqW68Is_Kj4&t=82s) at 01:22 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-katelyn-lesse-anthropic/transcript.md))
- Memory plus context editing measurably helps. “if we combined our memory tool with context editing, we saw a 39% bump in performance over o over the benchmark on our own internal evals.” — Katelyn Lesse, [*Evolving Claude APIs for Agents*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aqW68Is_Kj4&t=408s) at 06:48 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-katelyn-lesse-anthropic/transcript.md))
- LLMs are stateless; better tokens in, better tokens out, and past roughly 40% fill they get worse. “Around the 40% line is where you're going to start to see some diminishing returns depending on your task.” — Dex Horthy, [*No Vibes Allowed: Solving Hard Problems in Complex Codebases*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rmvDxxNubIg&t=365s) at 06:05 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-dex-horthy-humanlayer/transcript.md))
- Sub-agents are a context tool. “Sub aents are not for anthropomorphizing roles. They are for controlling context.” — Dex Horthy, [*No Vibes Allowed: Solving Hard Problems in Complex Codebases*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rmvDxxNubIg&t=365s) at 06:05 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-dex-horthy-humanlayer/transcript.md)) Amp says the same: “So sub agents are effectively a way to conserve and extend the context window of your main agent.” — Beyang Liu, [*Amp Code: Next-Generation AI Coding*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gvIAkmZUEZY&t=400s) at 06:40 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-beyang-liu-amp-code/transcript.md))
- Replit offloads state to files and memory rather than chasing longer windows. “from experience, we found that most of the tasks, even the more ambitious one, can be accomplished within the 200,000 tokens.” — Michele Catasta, [*Autonomy Is All You Need*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLhAA9yguwM&t=896s) at 14:56 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-michele-catasta-replit/transcript.md))
- Skills load only their metadata until needed. “That's why skills are progressively disclosed. At runtime, only this metadata is shown to the model just to indicate that he has the skill.” — Barry Zhang, Mahesh Murag, [*Don't Build Agents, Build Skills Instead*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CEvIs9y1uog&t=248s) at 04:08 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-barry-zhang-mahesh-murag-anthropic/transcript.md))
- Developers' distrust of AI code is mostly distrust of its context. “they said 80 80% of the time they don't trust the context that the LLM have” — Itamar Friedman, [*The State of AI Code Quality: Hype vs. Reality*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rgjF5o2Qjsc&t=835s) at 13:55 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-20-itamar-friedman-qodo/transcript.md))
- The system prompt itself is context that keeps being iterated. “these actually aren't just static. They are repeatedly iterated on.” — Aparna Dhinakaran, [*Continual System-Prompt Learning for Code Agents*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pP_dSNz_EdQ&t=23s) at 00:23 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-aparna-dhinakaran-arize/transcript.md))
- Netflix's research-plan-implement exists to keep the window clean. “when AI has a clear specification to follow, the context remains clean and focused.” — Jake Nations, [*The Infinite Software Crisis*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eIoohUmYpGI&t=797s) at 13:17 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-jake-nations-netflix/transcript.md))
- swyx named sub-agents against context rot as a conference-wide pattern. “you can also use sub agents to fight context rot.” — swyx, [*Opening Remarks (No More Slop)*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IoiHI7p12Ao&t=433s) at 07:13 ([transcript](../talks/2025-11-21-swyx-no-more-slop/transcript.md))

From the notes: day 1's first recurring theme is "Context is very important, driver of quality"; day 2 lists "subagents to context maintenance" and "antigravity has artifacts as their solution for memory and context"; Dex's "dumb zone" and "DO NOT OUTSOURCE THE THINKING" are in capitals.

### 4. The organisation, not the tool, is the constraint — 10 talks

Leadership day's centre of gravity. The gains show up only where roles, incentives, team shape and workflows change; mandates and tool rollouts alone produce 5–15%.

### 5. The product is the model trained in its own harness — 8 talks

Engineering day's centre of gravity. Cursor, OpenAI, Prime Intellect, Applied Compute, Cline, MiniMax and Meta all describe RL inside an environment that mirrors the product: tools, tasks and rewards. Harness and model are being co-designed.

## Limits

- **Auto-captions only.** All transcripts are YouTube's ASR ("cloud code" for Claude Code, "SLO" for slop, "VIP coding" for vibe coding, "Mahes" for Mahesh). Quotes keep these errors on purpose; check the video before reusing a quote in print.
- **One attendee's sample.** 37 talks from two of the summit's days; the workshop day and the afternoon expo-stage talks are not covered, and four scheduled sessions have no notes.
- **Two Whisper transcripts.** Their timestamps come from Whisper segments, and the first paragraph may begin a few seconds before speech. Joel Becker's live notes hold only slide photos, so that talk rests on the transcript alone.
- **Slides are machine-selected.** Frames were cropped from the speaker+slide composite, de-duplicated and capped at 15 per talk by even sampling, so some slides are missing and a few demo frames remain.
- **Claims were extracted per talk by model-assisted reading** (4–6 per talk) and clustered by hand; a talk's absence from a theme means it did not argue that point prominently, not that it never mentioned it.
- **Notes vs. transcripts.** The live notes are typed fast and uncorrected; where they disagree with a transcript, the transcript is the evidence.
